Filament takes up moisture from the air. In the hot nozzle the water turns to steam, which shows
as strings, oozing, a rough surface, small holes and weaker layers. Nylon and TPU need drying far more
often than PLA.

## Drying times

Elegoo's values, for a forced-air filament dryer. Never use a microwave or a kitchen oven.

| Material | Drying | Temperature | Time |
|---|---|---|---|
| PLA | recommended | 55 ± 5 °C | 6 h |
| PETG | recommended | 60 ± 5 °C | 8–10 h |
| TPU | mandatory | 70 ± 5 °C | 6–10 h |
| ASA | recommended | 70 ± 5 °C | 4–8 h |
| ABS | recommended | 70 ± 5 °C | 4–6 h |
| PC | mandatory | 80 ± 5 °C | 6–12 h |

For nylon, Prusa advises at least 4 hours below 90 °C. For anything else, use the values on the
spool's label.

## Storage

Straight after drying, seal the spool in a bag with fresh desiccant. Keep it cool, dry and dark:
ideally 15–25 °C below 30 % relative humidity, away from sunlight and heaters.
