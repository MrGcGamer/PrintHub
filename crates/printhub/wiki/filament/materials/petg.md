PETG is the common material for parts that have to work. Its layers bond well, so parts are
tough and waterproof, and it hardly shrinks, so large prints stay flat.

**Use it for** brackets, holders, clamps, boxes, mechanical and waterproof parts, indoors or out
below 80 °C.

**Avoid it for** fine detail, steep overhangs and long bridges, which it prints worse than PLA,
and for parts that need supports removed cleanly: they stick.

## Settings

| Profile | Nozzle | Bed | Fan | Flow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elegoo PETG | 250 (230–270) | 70 / 70 | 10–40 | 11 |
| Elegoo PETG HF | 240 (230–250) | 70 / 70 | 30–50 | 18 |
| Elegoo PETG PRO | 240 (230–270) | 70 / 70 | 10–40 | 5 |
| Elegoo PETG Translucent | 255 (230–270) | 70 / 70 | 10–35 | 10 |
| Elegoo Rapid PETG | 250 (230–270) | 70 / 70 | 30–80 | 18 |
| Elegoo PETG-CF | 250 (240–270) | 70 / 70 | 5–40 | 12 |
| Elegoo PETG-GF | 250 (240–270) | 70 / 70 | 5–40 | 10 |
| Elegoo PET-CF | 270 (260–290) | 100 / 70 | 10–40 | 8 |
| Generic PETG-CF | 240 (230–260) | 70 / 70 | 20–60 | 12 |
| Generic PET-CF | 280 (240–290) | 90 / 90 | 30–60 | 8 |
| Generic PET | 270 (250–280) | 90 / 90 | 20–60 | 12 |

The -CF and -GF profiles are filled with carbon or glass fibre; read
[Fibre-filled filament](/wiki/filament/materials/fibre-filled) before printing them. PET without
the G is harder to print and, according to Prusa, has no advantage over PETG.

## Printing it

- PETG strings, more so when wet; see [Drying](/wiki/filament/materials/drying).
- It can stick to a smooth PEI surface too well to come off undamaged. Prusa advises a textured
  sheet, or glue stick on a smooth one as a separator.
- More cooling keeps detail; less cooling bonds layers better and makes parts tougher.
