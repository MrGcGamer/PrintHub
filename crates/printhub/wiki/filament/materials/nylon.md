Nylon is extremely resilient. It resists abrasion, slides with little friction, bonds its layers
very well and takes heat and chemicals. Thick sections are strong; thin ones bend instead of
breaking.

**Use it for** gears, and parts that bend or wear.

**Avoid it** unless the filament can be kept dry. Nylon takes up water from the air, up to 10 %
of its weight, and prints with bubbles and a rough surface once it has.

## Settings

| Profile | Nozzle | Bed | Fan | Flow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Generic PA | 250 (240–290) | 100 / 100 | 80–100 | 8 |
| Generic PA6-CF | 280 (260–300) | 100 / 100 | 10–60 | 8 |
| Elegoo PAHT-CF | 290 (260–290) | 100 / 60 | 10–30 | 6 |

The -CF profiles are filled with carbon fibre; read
[Fibre-filled filament](/wiki/filament/materials/fibre-filled) first. Carbon-filled nylons warp
far less than plain nylon.

## Printing it

- Dry it before printing and store it sealed with desiccant. See
  [Drying](/wiki/filament/materials/drying).
- Nylon gives off a strong smell and ultrafine particles: print with the enclosure closed in a
  ventilated room.
- Plain nylon warps; the enclosure's warmth helps.
