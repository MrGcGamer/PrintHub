TPU is a flexible, rubber-like material. It is tough, resists wear and solvents, stays flexible in
the cold and hardly shrinks. Its number, such as 95A, is its Shore hardness: the lower the number,
the softer the filament and the harder it is to print.

**Use it for** washers, stamps, tyres, shoe soles, belts and phone sleeves.

**Avoid it for** models with overhangs, bridges or supports: it bridges badly, and supports are
hard to remove.

## Settings

| Profile | Nozzle | Bed | Fan | Flow |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Elegoo TPU 95A | 225 (200–250) | 35 / 35 | 100 | 3.2 |
| Elegoo Rapid TPU 95A | 230 (200–230) | 35 / 35 | 100 | 12 |

Plain TPU 95A is limited to about a quarter of Rapid TPU 95A's flow, so it prints more slowly.

## Loading it on the Centauri Carbon 2

TPU does not feed through the CANVAS like other filament. From Elegoo's
[TPU guide](https://wiki.elegoo.com/centauri-carbon-2-combo/how-to-print-tpu):

1. With the printer off, fit the TPU adapter to CANVAS channel 1 as the guide shows.
2. Push the TPU into the adapter's lower tube until it will not go further and has reached the
   five-way hub.
3. Switch on, go to *Filament › Edit* on the touchscreen, set the type to TPU, confirm, and tap
   *Load TPU*.

With auto-levelling on, the printer levels at 60 °C and then waits for the bed to cool to TPU's
35 °C, which can take 15 to 25 minutes before printing begins.

## Printing it

- Elegoo calls drying TPU mandatory; see [Drying](/wiki/filament/materials/drying).
- Expect strings, and design parts without overhangs where possible.
