Short carbon or glass fibres mixed into a plastic change how it behaves. Parts keep their shape
better, warp less, weigh less, and resist heat and pulling forces better. In exchange they break more easily
on impact and their layers bond less well. Some carbon-filled filaments are made mostly for
their look.

Each base material's page lists its filled profiles: [PLA](/wiki/filament/materials/pla),
[PETG and PET](/wiki/filament/materials/petg), [ABS](/wiki/filament/materials/abs),
[ASA](/wiki/filament/materials/asa), [PC](/wiki/filament/materials/pc) and
[nylon](/wiki/filament/materials/nylon).

## Printing it

> Fibres are highly abrasive: print them only with a hardened steel nozzle. The Centauri
> Carbon 2 comes with one, but PrintHub records only the [nozzle size](/wiki/printer/nozzle), so
> check any nozzle fitted since.

- Clogs are more likely. The first layer has to go down well, and a larger nozzle with thicker
  layers clogs less; Prusa puts the lower limit at a 0.4 mm nozzle with 0.2 mm layers.
- Cheap fibre filaments clog, ooze and snap more often.
